Molecular virology and virus–host interactions

Studying how viral infection reshapes host cells and drives disease.

The Tang Laboratory investigates medically important viruses through molecular virology, cell biology, host-response analysis, and disease-focused experimental models.

Explore research Meet the team

Research focus

The lab connects viral mechanisms with host pathways, including gene regulation, signal transduction, innate defense, organelle biology, and viral pathogenesis.

  • ✓Zika virus, dengue virus, hepatitis C virus, and cytomegalovirus biology
  • ✓Host responses including interferon signaling, lipid droplets, ER stress, and organelle remodeling
  • ✓Experimental systems spanning molecular assays, cell culture, and disease models

What we study

Mechanisms linking infection, host pathways, and disease outcomes

The lab’s work is organized around a shared question: how do viruses use, disrupt, or expose host-cell programs during infection?

Virus–host interaction

Defining how viral proteins and genomes engage host defenses, transcriptional regulation, and cell signaling.

Flavivirus pathogenesis

Investigating ZIKV and DENV infection, including lipid-droplet biology, ER stress, calcium signaling, and cardiac or neural injury models.

Innate antiviral defense

Studying interferon-driven pathways, pattern-recognition receptor signaling, ISG expression, and ISGylation during infection.

Herpesvirus biology

Exploring cytomegalovirus gene regulation, ND10/PML-related host defense, BAC-based viral tools, and vector-development concepts.
