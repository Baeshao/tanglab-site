Molecular virology · host response · viral pathogenesis

Studying how medically important viruses reprogram human cells.

The Tang Laboratory investigates virus–host interactions using molecular virology, cell biology, metabolic assays, imaging, and organoid-based models. Our work focuses on how viral infection changes cellular pathways and how those mechanisms can reveal new therapeutic targets.

About the laboratory

Mechanistic virology with disease-relevant models

Our research combines classical virology with modern cell and organoid systems to define how viruses alter host metabolism, organelle function, immune signaling, and cell physiology. The laboratory works across flaviviruses, hepatitis C virus, and human cytomegalovirus to understand shared and virus-specific mechanisms of pathogenesis.

Current directions

Research highlights

Zika virus and cardiac models

Using cardiomyocytes and cardiac organoids to study ZIKV infection, lipid droplet remodeling, and calcium signaling.

Viral control of metabolism

Profiling how viral infection changes lipid handling, organelle communication, and energy-linked host pathways.

Antiviral and vector strategies

Applying molecular cloning, BAC engineering, and host-pathogen assays to investigate viral replication and therapeutic potential.

Training environment

A focused laboratory for graduate research in virology

The lab supports projects that connect molecular mechanisms to disease-relevant phenotypes, with an emphasis on careful experimental design, reproducible assays, and clear biological interpretation.
