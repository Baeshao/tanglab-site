Virus–host interactions across compartments and scales

From viral gene regulation to metabolic rewiring, we study how viruses reshape the cell.

At Howard University College of Medicine, the Tang Laboratory investigates the host pathways that viruses encounter, exploit, and disrupt—from nuclear antiviral structures and gene regulation to organelle stress, lipid metabolism, innate defense, and disease pathogenesis.

Explore our research Read selected publications

A continuous mechanistic thread

The lab works across RNA and DNA virus systems, but the central question is consistent: what changes inside a host cell determine whether infection succeeds and how disease develops?

  • ✓CMV and herpesvirus biology: immediate-early proteins, PML/ND10, viral gene regulation, and intrinsic cellular defense.
  • ✓Flavivirus biology: ZIKV and DENV interactions with ER stress pathways, lipid droplets, organelle signaling, and pathogenesis.
  • ✓Host response and metabolism: interferon/ISG programs and metabolic regulators that shape the infected-cell state.

Scientific perspective

We follow mechanisms, not just virus names.

Tang Lab research connects molecular events inside infected cells with phenotypes that matter at the tissue and disease level. Three themes organize much of that work.

Nuclear control of viral infection

Long-standing work in the lab examines how DNA viruses interact with host nuclear structures and regulatory proteins, including PML/ND10, and how viral immediate-early proteins alter transcriptional control and intrinsic defense.

Explore CMV and herpesvirus biology

Organelle and metabolic remodeling

Current RNA-virus research asks how ER stress, the unfolded protein response, lipid droplets, mitochondrial metabolism, and cellular energy sensing are reorganized during infection.

Explore flavivirus pathogenesis

From mechanism to pathogenesis

The lab links cell-biological mechanisms to disease-relevant systems, including ZIKV-associated cardiac and neural injury and host-response pathways that influence viral replication and tissue dysfunction.

Current emphasis

Questions connecting stress, metabolism, and antiviral defense

How does infection redirect cellular energy?

Recent work from lab members examines AMPK-centered metabolic control together with lipid metabolism, autophagy, mitochondrial dynamics, oxidative stress, and innate immune signaling in flavivirus infection.

How do organelles become part of the viral life cycle?

Flavivirus research in the lab connects ER stress and unfolded protein response signaling with lipid-droplet remodeling and the membrane and metabolic demands of viral replication.

How do antiviral pathways alter disease outcomes?

Across the lab's work, intrinsic nuclear defenses, interferon signaling, ISG programs, and host-cell remodeling are studied as determinants of viral replication and pathogenesis.
