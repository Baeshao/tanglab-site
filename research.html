Research

Research programs

Tang Lab research centers on the molecular events that connect viral infection to host-cell dysfunction, immune responses, and disease.

Program 01

Virus–host interactions

The lab studies how viral infection causes disease by changing host-cell pathways. Major themes include gene regulation, signal transduction, molecular virology, cell biology, and cellular innate defense.

  • Viral protein–host factor interactions
  • Host-cell transcriptional and post-translational control
  • Intrinsic and innate cellular antiviral defenses

Program 02

ZIKV and DENV pathogenesis

The flavivirus program examines how Zika virus (ZIKV) and dengue virus (DENV) interact with host cells. Current public research themes include ZIKV protein biology, neutralization systems, lipid droplets, ER stress, and cardiac or neural disease mechanisms.

  • Cell-culture infection models and protein-expression systems
  • Lipid-droplet and organelle-remodeling analysis
  • ZIKV cardiac and neonatal pathogenesis models

Program 03

Innate antiviral defense

Lab publications and reviews address how pathogen-associated molecular patterns activate pattern-recognition receptors, type I interferon production, interferon-stimulated genes, and ISGylation during viral infection.

  • PAMP–PRR signaling and interferon response pathways
  • ISG expression and antiviral effector mechanisms
  • Host-targeted antiviral concepts

Program 04

CMV and herpesvirus biology

The lab has a long-standing interest in cytomegalovirus (CMV), ND10/PML biology, viral gene regulation, BAC engineering, and vector-based applications.

  • CMV immediate-early protein biology and host restriction factors
  • Viral bacterial artificial chromosome engineering
  • CMV-based vector and vaccine concepts

Approaches

Experimental toolkit

The lab integrates molecular virology and cellular methods with disease-focused models.

  • Molecular cloning and viral protein-expression systems
  • Quantitative PCR, western blotting, immunofluorescence, and subcellular analysis
  • Cell-culture infection assays, neutralization testing, and host-response readouts
  • Animal and organ-relevant models for viral pathogenesis questions
