Research

Mechanisms of virus–host interaction

Tang Lab investigates how HCV, flaviviruses, and herpesviruses alter host gene regulation, organelle function, metabolism, antiviral defense, and disease pathogenesis across molecular, cellular, and tissue-level models.

Research areas Virus–host interactions Flaviviruses / HCV ZIKV cardiac pathogenesis Antiviral defense CMV / Herpesviruses Approaches

Program 01

Virus–host interactions

The lab studies viral infection as a dynamic interaction between viral factors and host-cell regulatory systems. Across multiple virus families, the goal is to identify host pathways that determine replication efficiency, cellular stress responses, and disease-relevant phenotypes.

  • Viral protein–host factor interactions
  • Transcriptional and post-translational regulation during infection
  • Intrinsic and innate antiviral defenses
  • Organelle and signaling responses to viral replication

Program 02

Flavivirus and HCV pathogenesis and cellular remodeling

Current work on Zika virus (ZIKV), dengue virus (DENV), and hepatitis C virus (HCV) examines the cellular systems these RNA viruses reorganize to support infection. Tang Lab scholarship highlights ER stress and the unfolded protein response, lipid-droplet dynamics, metabolic reprogramming, mitochondrial homeostasis, and cellular energy sensing.

  • ER stress, unfolded protein response signaling, and replication-organelle biology
  • Lipid-droplet formation, degradation, and lipid mobilization
  • Metabolic regulation, autophagy, and mitochondrial dynamics
  • HCV replication and host-response biology
  • ZIKV cellular remodeling and disease mechanisms

Program 03

ZIKV cardiac pathogenesis

Tang Lab studies mechanisms of ZIKV-associated cardiac injury, linking virus-induced changes in host metabolism, signaling, and intercellular communication with cardiac dysfunction.

  • Metabolic remodeling in infected cardiomyocytes
  • Cardiac signaling and functional disruption
  • Connexin 43, gap-junction integrity, and intercellular communication
  • Cell and animal models of ZIKV-associated cardiac injury

Program 04

Innate and intrinsic antiviral defense

The lab studies host defense at several levels, from nuclear restriction mechanisms to pathogen sensing and interferon-dependent antiviral programs. Recent work reviews how PAMP–PRR signaling drives type I interferon production, ISG expression, and ISGylation, while earlier herpesvirus studies examine PML/ND10-associated intrinsic defense.

  • PAMP–PRR signaling and type I interferon responses
  • Interferon-stimulated genes and ISGylation
  • PML/ND10 and nuclear intrinsic-defense mechanisms
  • Host-directed antiviral concepts

Program 05

CMV, herpesvirus gene regulation, and viral engineering

A long-standing Tang Lab research direction examines cytomegalovirus (CMV) immediate-early proteins, viral gene regulation, and interactions with nuclear host-defense structures. This work also extends to bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) engineering and CMV-based vector concepts.

  • CMV immediate-early proteins and host restriction factors
  • PML/ND10 biology and viral counter-defense mechanisms
  • Viral BAC engineering and genetic manipulation
  • CMV-based vector and vaccine concepts

Approaches

Experimental toolkit

The lab combines molecular and cellular virology with disease-focused experimental systems to move between mechanism and phenotype.

  • Molecular cloning, viral genetics, and protein-expression systems
  • Quantitative PCR, immunoblotting, immunofluorescence, and subcellular analysis
  • Cell-culture infection, neutralization, and host-response assays
  • Organelle, metabolic, signaling, and intercellular-communication readouts
  • Cell and animal models for viral pathogenesis
